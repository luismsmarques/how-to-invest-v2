# Semana 1

## Segunda 31 Ago · The Arithmetic

```
WHAT ONE PIP IS ACTUALLY WORTH — IN RUPEES

A "pip" is the smallest standard price move in a currency pair. On EUR/USD it's 0.0001.

Most people learn what a pip is. Fewer work out what it costs them.

On EUR/USD, one pip is worth roughly:

  1 standard lot (100,000 units)  →  ₹955
  1 mini lot (10,000)             →  ₹95.50
  1 micro lot (1,000)             →  ₹9.55

So a 20-pip move — which on an ordinary day happens before lunch — is about ₹191 on a single micro lot. On a standard lot, ₹19,100.

That gap is the whole reason lot size decides more about an account than entry price does.

Educational only. Forex and CFDs are leveraged, high-risk products; most retail accounts lose money.
```

## Terça 1 Set · Rupee & Macro

```
WHAT ACTUALLY MOVES THE RUPEE

USD/INR doesn't move because of chart patterns. Four things do most of the work.

1. The dollar itself. When the dollar strengthens against everything, the rupee usually falls along with the crowd — nothing India did.

2. Oil. India imported 88.7% of its crude in 2025-26, according to figures placed before the Rajya Sabha. Every barrel is paid for in dollars, so a rising oil price means more dollars leaving the country.

3. Interest rate gaps. When money earns more abroad than it does here, it tends to travel.

4. The RBI. It buys and sells dollars from its reserves to smooth sharp moves — to reduce volatility, not to defend a particular level.

None of that is a reason to place a trade. It's a way to read a headline and understand why the number moved.
```

## Quarta 2 Set · Risk Lab

```
THE MATHS OF GETTING BACK TO EVEN

Losing and recovering are not symmetrical, and that catches almost everyone out.

  Lose 10%  →  you need +11% to break even
  Lose 25%  →  you need +33%
  Lose 50%  →  you need +100%
  Lose 75%  →  you need +300%

The hole deepens faster than the ladder lengthens.

This is why people who have been doing this a while talk about position size more than they talk about entries. A small loss is a rounding error. A large one changes what is mathematically available to the account for a long time afterwards.

₹10,000 that becomes ₹5,000 has to double just to be ₹10,000 again.
```

## Quinta 3 Set · Rules of the Game

```
FOREX AND THE LAW IN INDIA — THE SHORT VERSION

This is the part most channels skip.

For a resident of India, the routes into currency trading are limited:

• INR pairs on a recognised Indian exchange — NSE, BSE, MSE — through a SEBI-registered broker.
• Through an IFSC-registered broker in GIFT City, funded under the Liberalised Remittance Scheme.

Trading foreign currency pairs on an offshore OTC platform is not on that list. Under FEMA there is no category of "legal but unregulated", and the RBI publishes an Alert List of entities not authorised to deal in forex here.

The arithmetic we post works the same wherever you are. The rules don't.

Check the current Alert List and the LRS rules at rbi.org.in, or with a professional.
```

# Semana 2

## Segunda 7 Set · The Arithmetic

```
LOT SIZES, WITHOUT THE JARGON

A "lot" is just a quantity of currency. Three standard sizes:

  Standard lot  =  100,000 units
  Mini lot      =   10,000 units
  Micro lot     =    1,000 units

On most platforms these appear as 1.00, 0.10 and 0.01.

Gold works differently: a standard lot is 100 ounces, not 100,000 of anything.

The micro lot matters more than it looks. It's the smallest step of risk most platforms allow — the difference between adjusting exposure in fine increments and moving in tenfold jumps.

If an account can only afford whole micro lots, that isn't an obstacle to work around. It's the arithmetic describing where the account actually is.
```

## Terça 8 Set · Rupee & Macro

```
THE DOLLAR INDEX, AND WHY THE RUPEE LISTENS TO IT

DXY is an index of the US dollar against a basket of six currencies — mostly the euro, plus the yen, pound, Canadian dollar, Swedish krona and Swiss franc.

The rupee isn't in it. And yet USD/INR often moves with it.

The reason is plain enough: DXY measures dollar strength in general. When the dollar is strong against everything, it tends to be strong against the rupee too — for reasons that have nothing to do with India at all.

Worth knowing before blaming a domestic headline for a move. Sometimes the rupee didn't fall. The dollar rose.
```

## Quarta 9 Set · Risk Lab

```
LEVERAGE IS NOT BUYING POWER

1:500 leverage doesn't mean an account can afford 500 times more. It means the platform will allow it.

Two numbers get confused here, and they're unrelated:

  Margin — what gets locked up to hold the position
  Risk   — what is actually lost if the trade goes wrong

For 0.05 lots on EUR/USD at 1:500, the margin is around ₹1,113. A 20-pip move against that same position costs about ₹955 — almost as much as the margin, and calculated from something else entirely.

Margin is a deposit. Risk is what stop distance and lot size decide together.

High leverage doesn't raise risk by itself. It removes the thing that used to prevent you from taking it.
```

## Quinta 10 Set · Rules of the Game

```
HOW TO CHECK A PLATFORM — AND WHAT THE CHECK WON'T TELL YOU

The RBI keeps an Alert List of entities not authorised to deal in foreign exchange in India, or to operate an electronic trading platform for it. It's published at rbi.org.in and updated from time to time.

Two things are worth understanding about it.

1. Appearing on the list is a clear warning.
2. Being absent from it is not a clearance. The RBI says so directly: the list is not exhaustive, and an entity that doesn't appear on it should not be assumed to be authorised.

The more useful check runs the other way — the RBI's registers of authorised dealers and authorised electronic trading platforms. Those say who IS permitted.

The absence of a red flag is not a green one.
```

# Semana 3

## Segunda 14 Set · The Arithmetic · 🔗 link

```
POSITION SIZE, WORKED FROM START TO FINISH

The formula:

  Lots = (Balance × Risk%) ÷ (Stop in pips × Pip value per lot)

An example. A ₹1,00,000 account. 1% risked on one trade. A 20-pip stop on EUR/USD.

  1% of ₹1,00,000            =  ₹1,000
  Pip value, 1 standard lot  ≈  ₹955
  1,000 ÷ (20 × 955)         =  0.052 lots

Round DOWN to 0.05. Always down — rounding up quietly risks more than was decided.

Actual risk on 0.05 lots: ₹955.

Notice the order the formula puts things in. The loss is chosen first, and the position size falls out of it. Most people work the other way round and discover what they risked afterwards.

The 1–2% figure is a widely cited convention in trading literature, not a rule.

Calculator, if you'd rather not do it by hand:
howtoinvest.pro/forex/position-size-calculator/
```

## Terça 15 Set · Rupee & Macro

```
OIL, AND WHY IT LANDS ON THE RUPEE

India imported 88.7% of its crude in 2025-26. Domestic production has been falling for years while consumption has not.

Follow what that means mechanically.

Crude is priced and paid for in dollars. To buy it, importers sell rupees and buy dollars. More expensive oil, or more of it, means more rupees sold and more dollars bought — every single month, regardless of what anyone thinks about the rupee.

So an oil headline is often a currency headline wearing different clothes. It shows up in the trade deficit first, and in the exchange rate after.

This is context for reading the news, not a reason to trade it.
```

## Quarta 16 Set · Risk Lab

```
THE NUMBER WHERE THE ARITHMETIC RUNS OUT

One micro lot on EUR/USD, with a 20-pip stop, risks about ₹191.

For ₹191 to be 1% of an account, the account needs to be around ₹19,100.

Below that, the smallest position most platforms allow already risks more than 1% of what's there. At ₹5,000 that same trade is 3.8% of the account. At ₹2,000, it's 9.5%.

That isn't an argument for trading bigger. It's the division describing a real constraint — and the honest responses are a larger account or a tighter stop, not a larger position.

Nobody sells a course on this number. It's just what the arithmetic says when you ask it.
```

## Quinta 17 Set · Rules of the Game

```
HOW A SIGNAL SCAM ACTUALLY WORKS

The pattern repeats, which makes it recognisable.

1. A channel posts winning screenshots. The losing ones don't get posted.
2. Free signals for a while. Some of them win — enough to build trust.
3. A private message arrives. Nobody legitimate starts there.
4. A specific platform gets recommended, usually with a referral link and a "bonus".
5. Deposits go in easily. Withdrawals develop problems.

Two details give it away nearly every time: a promised win rate, and the DM.

Nobody from this channel will ever message you privately. If someone does using this name, it isn't us — report and block.

And any platform suggested by a stranger is worth checking against the RBI Alert List before anything else happens.

## Sexta 18 Set · The Week Ahead

```
WHY THE OVERLAP IS DIFFERENT

Between 17:30 and 21:30 IST, London and New York are open at the same time. It's the busiest window of the trading day.

What actually changes in it:

• More participants, so spreads tend to be at their tightest
• More volume behind each move, so prices travel further
• Economic releases from both the US and the UK land inside it

None of that makes the window safer. Faster moves are faster in both directions, and a stop is as easy to reach going down as a target is going up.

It's simply the part of the day where the market is most awake — worth knowing whether you're trading in it or deliberately avoiding it.

# Semana 4

## Segunda 21 Set · The Arithmetic

```
WHAT MARGIN ACTUALLY LOCKS UP

Margin is the deposit a platform holds while a position is open. It is not the amount at risk, and confusing the two is expensive.

For 0.05 lots on EUR/USD at 1:500 leverage, with price around 1.165, the margin works out at roughly ₹1,113.

Change only the leverage and watch what happens:

  1:500  →  ≈ ₹1,113
  1:200  →  ≈ ₹2,781
  1:100  →  ≈ ₹5,563

Same position. Same risk if it moves 20 pips against you — still about ₹955 in every row. Only the deposit changed.

That's the point worth taking away. Leverage changes what gets locked, not what gets lost.
```

## Terça 22 Set · Rupee & Macro

```
WHAT THE RBI IS ACTUALLY DOING IN THE CURRENCY MARKET

"RBI intervention" appears in headlines constantly, usually without explanation.

The mechanics are ordinary. The RBI holds foreign exchange reserves — dollars, mostly. When the rupee is falling quickly, it can sell dollars from those reserves and buy rupees, which adds demand for rupees. When the rupee is rising quickly, it can do the reverse and add to reserves.

The stated aim is to reduce disorderly volatility, not to defend a particular level. The RBI has said this repeatedly: no target rate, just less violence in getting wherever the rate is going.

Two things follow. Reserve figures are worth watching, because they show the cost of smoothing. And a quiet rupee isn't necessarily a calm one.
```

## Quarta 23 Set · Risk Lab

```
GOLD IS NOT A CURRENCY PAIR

It sits in the same platform, in the same list, with the same buy and sell buttons. The size behind it is not the same at all.

A standard gold lot is 100 ounces. At a gold price around $4,650 an ounce, that's roughly $465,000 of exposure from one line in an order ticket.

For comparison, a standard lot of EUR/USD is 100,000 units.

Gold also moves in larger daily ranges than most major pairs. Bigger notional and bigger swings arrive together, which is why the same lot size that feels routine on EUR/USD behaves very differently here.

If you trade gold at all, the position-size arithmetic isn't optional — it's the only thing standing between the account and the contract size.
```

## Quinta 24 Set · Rules of the Game

```
WHAT HAPPENS WHEN SOMETHING GOES WRONG

Most of the discussion about unauthorised platforms is about whether they pay out. The more important question is what exists if they don't.

With a SEBI-registered broker on an Indian exchange, there is a complaints process, a regulator with jurisdiction, and an exchange-level grievance mechanism.

With an offshore OTC platform, neither the RBI nor SEBI has jurisdiction over the transaction. There is no Indian regulator to escalate to, because the trade was never inside the Indian regulatory perimeter in the first place. The RBI has also warned that residents dealing on unauthorised platforms may face consequences under FEMA.

Frozen accounts and blocked withdrawals get discussed a lot. The absence of anywhere to complain gets discussed far less, and it's the part that matters when it happens.

Verify current rules at rbi.org.in and sebi.gov.in, or with a professional.
```
